# Fine tokens trade labels instead of adding signal

*Research report, 2026-09-22, written after the first three runs of the [stride-16 investigation](../../history/stride16-feature-fusion.md). Its recommended per-scale readout was then run as `fusion-scaleheads` and tied (+0.0006 macro against `k_05`); the line is closed.*

The stride-16 line is not missing a hidden large gain. Every measurement so far, internal and external, says stride-16 features of a fine-tuned ReXNet-200 at 0.625 mm mostly substitute for stride-32 features: they shift AUC from peripheral and whole-joint labels toward the menisci rather than adding new information. Four numbers make the case. Deep-branch dropout reproduced truncation's per-label split almost exactly (menisci +0.007 vs +0.0063, the other eight −0.0077 vs −0.0073). Scale-diverse ensemble pairs beat same-scale pairs by only about +0.001. Even a meniscus gain kept with zero cost elsewhere is worth only **about +0.0012 to +0.0017 macro** (2 of 12 labels). And `fusion-sum` already reached +0.0011, which is roughly the ceiling that the ensemble evidence implies. The investigation did miss four things. An earlier stride-16 path was already rejected for the same reason (−0.0022, the path shrank during training). Every 196-token arm was scored at τ 1 instead of the deployed τ 0.75, although bag size and τ interact. The per-label "costs" are partly noise, and part of the PF OA loss is specific to the `k_05` reference. And the fusion design (a token-level sum feeding one shared attention) is the family the literature shows lets the dominant branch win. My recommendation is to run one free check, the τ 0.75 re-score of the existing checkpoints, and then close the line. The only training run with a published rationale is per-scale readouts with fixed-weight per-scale losses. I estimate its chance of beating `k_05` by more than +0.002 at roughly 15% (speculation, low confidence). Real fine detail belongs to the end-phase "bigger images" run, where 0.5 mm pixels carry acquired signal that token pitch cannot.

## Five sidelined branches point to one mechanism

Read together, the three stride-16 arms tell a consistent story. Truncation (14×14 stride-16 tokens, stage 5 removed) lost **−0.0042 macro** at ×4, with meniscus +0.0063 and the other eight labels −0.0073 (PF OA −0.032, ACL −0.010) ([truncation result](../../validation/results/pooling-and-attention/2026-09-22-stride16-truncation-result.md)). `fusion-sum` recovered ACL and PF OA and tied at +0.0011 (paired 95% −0.0023 to +0.0044). Zeroing its fine term cost −0.0032 but **left the menisci unchanged**: the fine term's contribution sat in lateral OA (−0.0167), medial OA (−0.0087), Baker's (−0.0081) and effusion (−0.0052), not in the labels that motivated the work ([fusion result](../../validation/results/pooling-and-attention/2026-09-22-stride16-fusion-result.md)). Deep-branch dropout at p = 0.25 then pushed the fused model back to truncation's operating point: menisci up, the rest down, macro −0.0038 against `fusion-sum`. The evidence therefore says **"substitute that trades labels,"** not "unused complementary information waiting to be unlocked" (inference, medium-high confidence).

The investigation's own docs cite only CheXtransfer, FPN and the crop-era scout. They omit an earlier direct precedent: the medial-lateral campaign already read ReXNet `features.10` (stride 16, 257 channels, 14×14) as "shared local evidence" through anatomical attention. That run lost **−0.0022 macro** and **−0.0145 on lateral meniscus**, and the model shrank the path during training (mean |evidence| 0.130 → 0.050, meniscus vector norm 0.164 → 0.064) ([medial-lateral findings](../../../autoresearch/campaigns/medial-lateral-transfer/findings.md)). It is the fifth documented case of a secondary pathway being sidelined by the deep ReXNet path. The regional branch took 4.94% of attention and removing it cost −0.0007 ([per-plane fusion diagnosis](../../validation/results/pooling-and-attention/2026-09-13-per-plane-fusion-usage-diagnosis.md)). Learned-zoom 0.42 mm glimpses took 2% of attention and removing them cost −0.0002 ([learned-zoom result](../../validation/results/crops-and-views/2026-09-16-learned-zoom-result.md)). Scout coarse tokens learned only when the fine pathway was weak ([shared-crops result](../../validation/results/crops-and-views/2026-09-16-shared-crops-result.md)). Across all five cases, forcing or gating usage has never produced a macro gain.

The ensemble numbers bound what fusion can reach. On the current split, same-scale pairs gain about +0.0030 over the pair mean, and k_05+stride16 gains +0.0040. Against `k_05` alone, that pair is only +0.0019, which generic variance reduction explains nearly on its own. That leaves **about +0.001 of prediction-level scale complementarity**, the same size as the loss-diversity bump seen earlier (+0.0009 over seed trios) ([loss-ensemble simulation](../../validation/results/labels-and-losses/2026-09-21-loss-ensemble-simulation.md)). k_05+fusion-sum gains +0.0029 over its mean, which is a same-recipe-sized gain and fits a fusion model whose fine branch is barely used. Within the correlated-views regime, Du et al. find that averaging per-branch predictions matches or beats joint fusion: UCF-101 86.8 vs 84.4, ModelNet40 91.9 vs 91.7 ([Du et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01233)). Their paper also shows joint training can beat averaging through "paired features" on other datasets. So the +0.001 is a soft ceiling, not a hard one, but `fusion-sum`'s +0.0011 already sits at it.

Noise sets how much of this one run can resolve. Macro run-to-run SD is 0.0008–0.0018, control pairs have spanned 0.0026–0.0034, and the docs state that one run per setting "resolves differences of roughly 0.005, not 0.002" ([2026-09-18 overnight](../../validation/results/campaigns-and-queues/2026-09-18-overnight-results.md)). Per-label SD across runs is 0.0045 ([run-to-run spread](../../validation/results/campaigns-and-queues/2026-09-19-untried-hyperparameter-queue.md)). Same-seed divergence moved ACL +0.0166 and PF OA −0.0170 on 802 studies whose inputs did not change ([oblique result](../../validation/results/sampling/2026-09-22-oblique-groups-result.md)). The meniscus direction is still the most credible per-label signal, because two mechanisms (truncation and deepdrop) reproduced it and deepdrop's CI excludes zero. Its macro value is capped at +0.0012–0.0017 even if every other label held.

## The literature blames the fusion design, not the operator

Swapping the operator is the lowest-value change. In every head-to-head found, sum and concatenation land within about 0.5 point of each other. Soft gates (SK, AFF) add 0.5–1 point inside backbones, but are not anti-dominance devices, and EfficientDet's learned fusion weights stayed near balance ([Dai et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14082); [Li et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.06586); [Tan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09070)). On multimodal benchmarks, sum, concat and FiLM all sat at or below the best single stream ([Peng et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15332)). A gate or concat on the current token sum should be expected to tie (evidence from other domains, medium confidence).

What does make a secondary branch count is giving it **its own supervised pooling path**. In CrossViT, only cross-attention with per-branch readouts gave the fine branch non-trivial standalone accuracy: 47.2% against ≤7.6% for token-level or class-token fusion. It also gave the best fused result, 81.0 against 80.2–80.3, and the authors note that under other fusions the coarse branch "dominates... by diminishing the effect of complementary S-branch." The pairing with a 4× token gap, the one that matches ours, was worse than the 12/16-pixel pairing ([Chen et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)). ZoomMIL pools each magnification with its own gated attention and sums the pooled vectors; a single shared attention dropped weighted F1 from 68.1 to 59.6 ([Thandiackal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12454)). The closest analogue to our ablation comes from CS-MIL's toy data, where the signal exists only at the fine scale. There, concatenation and mean fusion **ignored the fine-only signal entirely** (AUC 0.464 and 0.443), while a per-location softmax over scales recovered it (0.963) ([Deng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00216)). Pathology multi-scale MIL gains of 1–3 AUC points come from genuinely different fields of view with frozen features. Ours are two depths of one fine-tuned CNN on one field of view, so those gains overstate what to expect here.

The balancing literature explains why deepdrop hurt. Deepdrop is the "modality dropout" baseline. In independent re-runs, methods that suppress the dominant branch (modality dropout, OGM, AGM, Greedy) push the dominant branch's own accuracy below an equal-weight auxiliary-loss baseline (CREMA-D audio 63.44 → 56.99–60.69) ([Wei & Hu 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17730)). The Diagnosing paper says these methods "disturb the training of well-learnt modality" ([Wei et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09705)). Additive fixes preserve the dominant path. These include per-branch auxiliary losses (MMPareto's "uniform" baseline: CREMA-D 71.10 vs 66.13 for one joint loss), G-Blend's per-head weighting, and UMT distillation. G-Blend also shows that equal-weight auxiliary losses can hurt (Kinetics 70.8 vs 71.7), and that learned loss weights collapse back to naive training ([Wang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12681)). Nesting weakens the transfer further. Our fine map is the input to the deep map, so the setting is closer to deep supervision, which yields gains of a few tenths of a point ([Lee et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5185)), than to two sensors. On correlated same-source views, balancing yields about 0–2 points. On CheXpert frontal+lateral it cost −2.5 AUC points (secondary source) ([MultiFair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07328)). Head2Toe adds that intermediate layers help mainly for frozen backbones on out-of-domain tasks ("does not improve in-domain generalization") ([Evci et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03529)). Full fine-tuning is the regime where they matter least.

| Design family | Addresses branch dominance? | Evidence | Fit to our failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sum / concat / scalar or channel gate | No | Within ~0.5 pt of each other everywhere | Expect a tie (medium confidence) |
| Weak-branch forcing: branch dropout, OGM/AGM gradient scaling | Yes, by suppressing the strong branch | Lose to additive aux baselines in re-runs | Already observed: deepdrop −0.0038 |
| Per-scale pooling + readout, fused after pooling (ZoomMIL, CrossViT) | Yes, each scale is directly supervised | +0.8 pt CrossViT; +3.2 F1 vs concat in ZoomMIL | Best structural match (speculative for knee) |
| Fixed-weight per-branch aux losses (MMPareto uniform, G-Blend) | Yes, additively | Strong baseline in re-runs; hurt on Kinetics | Pairs with per-scale readouts |
| Per-location softmax over scales (CS-MIL) | Yes | Recovered a fine-only signal in toy data | Smaller code change; untested for nested scales |
| One mixed bag of fine + coarse tokens (the "concatenated scales" follow-up) | Weakly | DSMIL mixed bag +1.5 AUC; CS-MIL toy shows the fine signal ignored | Bag-size and τ risks with 196 fine tokens crowding 49 deep ones |

## Knee fine detail comes from pixels and focus, not token pitch

A 14×14 grid at 0.625 mm halves token pitch without adding a single pixel. Receptive-field probes show the gain in locality is modest: the share of a token's input sensitivity from the opposite image half falls from 0.30–0.32 at stride 32 to only 0.23–0.24 at stride 16 ([medial-lateral findings](../../../autoresearch/campaigns/medial-lateral-transfer/findings.md)). Dilated Residual Networks show that lowering output stride helps classification (ResNet-34 top-1 error 27.73 → 24.81) only when the later layers are trained to operate at that resolution. Reading an intermediate map from a stride-32-pretrained network is a different intervention ([Yu et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09914)). Whole-network token coarsening tends to help classification, as with PiT-S's 80.9% against ViT-S's 79.8% at lower FLOPs ([Heo et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16302)). That fits truncation's −0.0042.

The knee evidence with large effects is about **where the model looks and how many acquired pixels it sees**. In a controlled OAI meniscal-tear ablation, medial anterior-horn AUC rose from 0.74 on the full volume to 0.87 with an ROI crop and to 0.94 with an auxiliary bounding-box loss on the full volume. The weakest regions gained most, and the lateral meniscus barely moved ([Frontiers 2021 / PMC8675251](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8675251/)). Top RSNA 2024 lumbar solutions all localized, then classified anatomy-sized crops at 192–384 px ([2nd place repo](https://github.com/brendanartley/RSNA-2024-Competition)). Chest X-ray resolution sweeps show small findings (nodules, emphysema) peaking at 448–512 px while large findings plateau at 256–320 ([Sabottke & Spieler 2020](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/ryai.2019190015)). No knee study isolates pixel resolution. Localization labels are unavailable here, and the project's own crop and learned-placement campaigns already closed at ≤0.005, with geographic trade-offs ([crop-selection history](../../history/crop-selection-investigation.md)).

The project's measurements agree. Only 23% of series keep their full acquired band at 0.625 mm, against 59% at 0.5 mm, and finer than about 0.5 mm adds almost nothing ([input-resolution research](input-resolution.md)). Coarsening to about 0.875 mm cost lateral meniscus −0.1057 and MCL −0.0671 ([learned-zoom result](../../validation/results/crops-and-views/2026-09-16-learned-zoom-result.md)). Every crop run showed the same geography: central, thin structures up and peripheral or whole-joint labels down. Examples are two 160-px crops (medial meniscus +0.0114, Baker's −0.0194) ([two-crops result](../../validation/results/crops-and-views/2026-09-15-two-crops-result.md)) and crop224 (ACL +0.0081, fracture −0.0142) ([crop224 result](../../validation/results/crops-and-views/2026-09-16-crop224-result.md)). The stride-16 per-label split is that same geography reproduced without new pixels: finer local evidence at the expense of context. The input-resolution doc also ranks depth sampling (triplets about 17 mm apart at ×1) as a larger gap than in-plane resolution for menisci. None of this contradicts the line's results. It explains why a token-pitch lever at fixed pixels has a small ceiling (inference, medium confidence).

### A dilated stride-16 backbone costs three times the backbone

*Measured 2026-09-25.* Dilating ReXNet-200's last stage (`output_stride=16` in timm) keeps the
pretrained weights and gives a 14×14 grid from the whole network, unlike truncation or fusion. At
the training shape (208 triplets of 3×224×224, bf16 autocast, RTX 3090) one forward and backward
took 0.997 s and peaked at 24.0 GiB, against 0.333 s and 18.6 GiB at stride 32
(`scripts/adhoc/benchmark_output_stride.py`). The backbone is about 86% of the control's
0.388 s training step (`report-attributes-control`, 1,709 s over 4,400 steps), so a run would take
roughly 2.7× as long, and the full model would not fit in 24 GiB without backbone checkpointing
or a smaller image budget. It adds token pitch but no pixels, at nearly twice the cost of the
[bigger-images](../../backlog/untried-ideas.md#views-and-fields) run (1.61× GMACs, 9×9 grid, real 0.5 mm
pixels), which dominates it. Not a candidate at the current budget.

## Four gaps that could move the verdict, only one by 0.002

**Temperature and bag size at the deployed operating point (evidence for the interaction; its size on these arms is unknown).** All stride-16 comparisons were scored at ×4 **τ 1**, but deployment is ×4 τ 0.75 (or ×5 τ 0.70). τ 0.75 is worth +0.0024 on frozen checkpoints ([attention temperature](../../validation/results/pooling-and-attention/2026-09-17-attention-temperature.md)) and +0.0025 at ×4 on the current split ([combined-labels run](../../validation/results/labels-and-losses/2026-09-21-combined-labels-first-run.md)). The same doc shows larger bags lose through weaker evidence stealing attention mass, a loss that sharper τ removes. The 196-token arms put about 20k tokens into each per-label softmax, against about 5k for `k_05`. Models are also known to scale differently from ×1 to ×4 (`oblique` went from −0.0015 at ×1 to +0.0003 at ×4). `fusion-sum`'s own swing from −0.0002 at ×1 to +0.0011 at ×4 fits this. This is the only gap that could plausibly move `fusion-sum`'s margin by 0.001–0.002, and checking it costs no training.

**Reference and noise (evidence).** The reference was the existing `k_05` run, not the fresh control the original plan specified. It was trained on a different commit, and training is not deterministic. PF OA is down against `k_05` in every recent comparison, including the unrelated oblique run (−0.017), and `k_05` had the best PF OA on the k-curve ([class-balance result](../../validation/results/labels-and-losses/2026-09-21-class-balance-exponent.md)). Part of the "other labels pay" story is therefore probably regression to the mean on one reference (inference, medium confidence). This softens the per-label costs but does not help macro, because the meniscus ceiling still caps any gain.

**Budget and learning rates (evidence for the facts; the effect is speculation).** `fusion-sum` selected epoch 10 with its ×1 curve still rising (0.8976 → 0.8988), while `k_05` peaked at epoch 8. The new `deep_projection` trains at head LR. A binding budget penalizes the arm with the new path, but lengthening training changes the control as well, so this caveat does not justify a run.

**Geometry and cost (evidence the checks are missing).** The anatomical bias uses a 20 mm tanh scale chosen on the 7×7 grid, and its behavior on 14×14 anchors is unrecorded. Full inference time for 196 tokens × about 104 triplets per study was never measured on Kaggle T4s, and the efficiency prize prices runtime. Neither can create a gain. Both would matter only if an arm were about to be adopted.

## The recommended last check and run (at the time)

The first recommended step needed no training: re-score `k_05`, `fusion-sum`, `stride16` and `fusion-deepdrop25` at the **fixed deployed setting, ×4 τ 0.75**, and compare paired against `k_05`. The τ is fixed rather than tuned per arm to avoid selection optimism. It is GPU inference on frozen checkpoints. The decision rule was simple. If `fusion-sum` stays below +0.002 over `k_05`, which is the likely outcome, the evidence said fusion had reached its ensemble-implied ceiling and the line closes. If it clears +0.002 with the 80% lower bound above zero, fusion becomes an adoption candidate, pending a T4 inference-time measurement.

If one training run was to be spent, the best-evidenced design was **per-scale readouts with fixed-weight per-scale losses**. The deep stream keeps its native 7×7 stride-32 tokens (49, as in `k_05`) with its own per-label attention. The fine stream pools its 196 stride-16 tokens with separate per-label attention. The final logit is the sum of the two per-label logits, and training adds equal fixed-weight BCE on each stream's own logits (MMPareto "uniform"; not learned, per G-Blend). This keeps the deep path exactly as it is and never subtracts from it. It gives the fine branch the directly supervised path that CrossViT and ZoomMIL show is necessary. It also lets each label weight scales differently without hand-tuning. It was recommended over the queued "concatenated scales" follow-up, because separate softmaxes avoid 196 fine tokens crowding 49 deep ones and the τ confound.

Its risks are documented. Equal-weight auxiliary losses hurt on Kinetics. An auxiliary objective on this shared backbone has hurt unrelated labels before: a contrastive term cost the other eight −0.0071 ([medial-lateral findings](../../../autoresearch/campaigns/medial-lateral-transfer/findings.md)). And the ensemble ceiling argument predicts roughly +0.001. My estimate is a gain of **+0.000 to +0.003 over `k_05`, with about a 15% chance of exceeding +0.002** (speculation, low confidence). One run cannot resolve 0.002 anyway, so the result would only be decisive if it came in large.

Several options were judged not worth running. Further deep-branch dropout rates and OGM/AGM-style gradient scaling would repeat the suppression trade-off. Sum→concat or sum→gate swaps are operators the literature puts within noise. UMT distillation from the truncated model uses a teacher whose menisci gain was itself inside noise. All three had low expected value.

## Conclusion

Fine detail matters for this task, and nothing so far argues otherwise; the stride-16 map at 0.625 mm is simply the wrong carrier for it. Token pitch without new pixels buys locality that the backbone's receptive field largely erases. The only label group that responds is worth at most about +0.0015 macro, and `fusion-sum` already sits at the complementarity ceiling that the pair ensembles reveal. The practical reframing is that scale diversity has now been priced, and it belongs to the end phase. There, a stride-16 or fusion checkpoint is worth about +0.001 more than a second seed as an ensemble member, and the "bigger images" run at 0.5 mm / 280 px changes both pixels and grid (9×9). That run is where finer tokens meet genuinely finer signal.

One lesson should carry forward. This project has now sidelined a secondary pathway five times, and it has never turned a forced pathway into a macro gain. Any future proposal that adds a branch beside the dominant ReXNet path should come with a per-scale-supervised readout designed in from the start. It should also carry a written ensemble-derived ceiling before a GPU hour is spent. With that ceiling calculated up front, this investigation would have been sized at about +0.001 before the first run.
