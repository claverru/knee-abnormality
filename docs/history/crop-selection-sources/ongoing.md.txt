What the runs established (single seed, fold 0):

- **Zoom helps central targets; the losses are at the edges.** Per-label against `00-baseline`:
  ACL +0.0111, synovitis +0.0077, medial OA +0.0057; lateral meniscus −0.0102, fracture −0.0085,
  MCL −0.0064, Baker's cyst −0.0039. Uncropped canvases are mostly edge slices, and fracture pairs
  are the most expensive
  ([pair costs](validation/target-level-diagnostics.md#cost-of-a-reversed-pair)). Resolution at a
  fixed field of view is untested ([input resolution](research/input-resolution.md)).
- **At 224 pixels, slice coverage matters more than per-label targeting.** Score follows how many
  canvases ReXNet-200 sees: all (`00-baseline`) 0.8928; 75% (crop224, per-label) 0.8916–0.8917;
  about 13 of a study's 51 validation canvases (shared, against about 38 for per-label) 0.8796;
  the same coverage pooled privately (label-private) 0.8451. Four crops per label at inference
  recover 0.003 of shared's 0.012 gap to per-label. This is a cross-run pattern, not a controlled
  run ([shared crops](validation/2026-09-16-shared-crops-result.md#reading)).
- **Selection adds little because the value map is weak.** Learned placement beat uniform and
  centred 160-pixel crops by about 0.005, a gap inflated by training on learned crops
  ([placement ablation](validation/2026-09-15-crop-selection-result.md#placement-ablation)). On
  the label-private checkpoint with shared pooling, attention beat random selection by 0.004, and
  a label's attention on its chosen canvas has a normalised entropy of 0.97–0.99
  ([probe](validation/2026-09-16-label-private-crops-result.md#selection-signal-probe)). In
  per-label, crop centres of studies with and without a finding differ by only 0.1–3.6 mm
  ([placement](validation/2026-09-16-per-label-crops-result.md#placement)).
- **A trained coarse pool makes placement study-specific only while fine pooling is weak.**
  Label-private's gated coarse pool reached 0.82 (other runs' drafts ended at 0.61–0.71), and its
  crop centres of positive and negative studies differ by up to 14.5 mm. With shared fine pooling
  the pool fell back to 0.65, and detail labels settled on a fixed lower position.
- **Private fine pooling starves ReXNet-200.** Label-private lost 0.047 to per-label, most on the
  menisci and ligaments; more crops or shared pooling at inference recover at most 0.004. The gate
  (0.505–0.537) and label FiLM (mean |scale| about 0.02) barely moved.
- **Misses can be recognition failures.** A detected fracture relied on crops other labels chose;
  a missed one lay inside other labels' crops and was not recognised
  ([renders](validation/2026-09-16-per-label-crops-result.md#visual-review-of-fracture-cases)).

### Open problems and options

Each option states whether a measured result backs it; otherwise it is a hypothesis. Options
marked *glimpse set* are parts of the recommended
[learned glimpse sets](#candidate-next-runs) run (run 1). Those marked *in run 0* are implemented,
in the form described there, by learned zoom, which awaits training.

#### Coverage and set selection

Independent per-label choices collapse onto favourite slices (shared crops: 24 crops on about 13
canvases), and fixed rules (one crop per canvas, plane spread, label quotas, the 75% fraction)
stand in for a set-level objective.

- **Label-weighted facility location over a shared budget** (*glimpse set*, *in run 0* as
  greedy selection without a per-label scale preference; hypothesis). Each
  label has a demand on each scout token. A glimpse covers the tokens inside its footprint,
  weighted by a learned per-label scale preference, and partially covers similar tokens elsewhere
  through per-study latent clusters, so redundancy is judged by appearance and crossing planes
  need no millimetre rule (millimetre overlap is meaningful only between parallel crops). Greedy
  maximisation of the total covered demand fills the budget and replaces the fixed rules; a
  redundant glimpse has no gain by construction. Choosing glimpses that act as centroids of a
  label's feature clusters is weighted k-medians, which greedy facility location approximates.
- **k-DPP selection policy** (*glimpse set*; hypothesis). A determinantal point process with
  kernel = demand quality × feature similarity samples diverse sets with exact probabilities, so
  exploration and policy gradients are consistent rather than bolted onto a greedy rule; with
  64-dimensional low-rank features it stays cheap for about 3,600 candidates per study. The simpler
  alternative is stochastic greedy selection: a softmax over marginal gains with an annealed
  temperature (*in run 0* without annealing: Gumbel-max on log gain).
- **Budget matched to the champion's compute** (*glimpse set*; hypothesis): the canvas count at
  validation (about 51 at 2× images); in training a cap of 24 ReXNet-200 glimpses per study
  including exploration (for example 16 chosen plus 8 exploration), the setting that fit in
  22.5 GiB, since training studies average about 25 canvases and heavy studies have many more.
  The model chooses context or detail per slice. Measured limit: a GPU profile of the eight heaviest studies peaked
  at 21.3 GiB with 224-pixel crops on 75% of canvases and ran out of memory with crops on all of
  them ([crop224](validation/2026-09-16-crop224-result.md#setup)).
- **Per-slice canvas centring** (hypothesis; a preprocessing change): superior axial slices sit
  off-centre because every canvas is centred on one study point
  ([fracture review](validation/2026-09-16-per-label-crops-result.md#visual-review-of-fracture-cases)).

#### Scale and in-plane precision

One scale serves all labels, although zoom helped central targets and hurt peripheral ones. A
224-pixel crop covers 14 of 19 scout rows, so it can move only 16 mm up or 24 mm down from the
canvas centre. Measured: 224-pixel crops scored 0.004473 above one 160-pixel crop per canvas and
improved every target except medial meniscus and medial OA
([crop224](validation/2026-09-16-crop224-result.md#reading)).

- **Glimpses at two scales** (*glimpse set*, *in run 0* with one centred context glimpse per
  canvas at 160 pixels and 0.875 mm instead of 224 pixels at 0.625 mm; hypothesis). A glimpse is
  (canvas, cell, scale):
  detail 224 px at 0.42 mm (94 mm, movable about ±28 mm) or context 224 px at 0.625 mm (140 mm,
  the champion's view). Canvases are prepared at 0.42 mm (360 px for 150 mm, 1.44× today's
  pixels); the scout and the context glimpses read GPU downsamples. A scale embedding marks
  glimpse tokens, and fine tokens map to the nearest scout cell. Evidence for the detail spacing:
  0.417 mm keeps the full acquired band in 85% of series against 59% at 0.5 mm, but stored pixels
  are about 2× interpolated, and the same research concludes that finer than about 0.5 mm adds
  almost nothing ([input resolution](research/input-resolution.md)).
- **Training-only crop jitter** (hypothesis): ±1 scout cell (±8 mm), in whole cells so fine tokens
  stay on coarse cells; tests whether findings sit inside the crop with margin.
- **Per-plane spacing** is tracked in [future work](future-work.md#per-plane-geometry).

#### Selection signal

A glimpse is a discrete choice with no gradient, and the model only sees the outcome of the
glimpses it took. One attention now serves both pooling, where a flat average works, and
selection, which needs peaks. The gated recipe's fine softmax renormalises over the chosen crops
only, so a chosen crop is never compared with unchosen regions; per-label, the best crop run,
pooled coarse and fine tokens in one softmax. Learned selectors are reported to collapse
([research](research/learned-crop-selection.md#learned-patch-selection-in-the-literature)). All
options below are hypotheses.

- **A demand head separate from the pooling attention** (*glimpse set*, *in run 0*): a small
  per-label head on scout tokens.
- **Union pooling with demand in the logits** (*glimpse set*, *in run 0*): coarse and glimpse tokens in one
  softmax per label, each glimpse token's logit adding its glimpse's log-demand, so demand
  competes with unchosen regions.
- **Per-label removal credit** (*glimpse set*): how much each label's BCE rises when a glimpse's
  tokens are removed from pooling, computed with pooling passes only (no backbone recompute).
  Redundant glimpses score near zero, so credit also rewards diversity. For a matched pair the
  members overlap, so removing one barely matters while the other stays; the pair is judged by a
  swap instead: label t prefers A when its BCE is lower with A pooled and B removed than with B
  pooled and A removed. The pairwise loss replaces the policy-gradient reward of the first draft;
  the k-DPP or stochastic greedy selection still provides exploration.
- **Matched exploration pairs with a pairwise loss** (*glimpse set*, the recommended signal; the
  idea behind RLOO and GRPO baselines: judge each sample against alternatives for the same input).
  - Besides the B chosen glimpses, training adds E exploration glimpses, each a sibling of a
    chosen one that differs in one decision: same slice at another position, the neighbouring
    slice, or the other scale. All B+E go through ReXNet-200 (E=8 is about +33% backbone cost,
    training only). Under a k-DPP, a pair is a DPP sample against its sibling.
  - A Bradley–Terry loss, −log σ(s_A − s_B), on the pair's demand-derived selection scores takes
    its preference from the swap credit. Pairs share the study, so study-level noise cancels.
  - It is a second loss term, but its targets come from the task BCE on the same batch and it
    touches only the selector; the classifier is still trained by the BCE alone.
- **Glimpse-outcome predictor** (JEPA-like world model; the fallback if pairwise credit is too
  noisy). A small head on scout tokens predicts the ReXNet-200 embedding a glimpse would produce
  at any candidate, trained on every glimpse taken, exploration included. Selection can then
  estimate each candidate's effect on each label's logits without running ReXNet-200. It never
  feeds the classifier. The strongest and most expensive of these options.

#### Latent space

Facility location and the k-DPP judge similarity in feature space, so the latent space matters
more than the algorithm. The scout is ImageNet-pretrained, and the class-activation probe chased
bright superficial signal, so similarity may track intensity rather than anatomy (hypothesis).
Compute similarity before the plane and contrast embeddings are added, or those split clusters
trivially.

- **Self-supervised scout pretraining (DINO)** (hypothesis; a parallel track). DINO's multi-crop
  matches this design: global views are scout canvases, local views are crops, and a
  student–teacher agreement loss teaches the scout what crops contain. Pretraining the scout on
  the roughly 175k training canvases without labels would give an anatomy-aware latent space for
  facility location or the k-DPP. A separate pretraining run of a few hours (estimate); the
  classifier's loss is unchanged.

#### Recognition and fusion

The scout and crop branches meet only in attention pooling, a missed fracture lay inside other
labels' crops, and label FiLM barely moved (see the findings above).

- **Branch fusion, global to local** (hypothesis; later): add a zero-initialised 1×1 projection of
  the crop's 14×14 scout cells (624 channels) to ReXNet-200's `features.10` (257 channels,
  stride 16), and scale and shift the same activations with a vector pooled over the whole canvas,
  keeping selection on the unfused scout. The cells align exactly only while crops share the
  scout's 0.5 mm spacing. It targets recognition and edge context
  ([GLNet](research/learned-crop-selection.md#global-local-branch-fusion), from dense, fully
  supervised tasks).
- **Crop-to-overview fusion** (local to global; hypothesis).

0.0024 over fine-only pooling, and the gates ended at 0.47–0.49
([probes](validation/2026-09-16-shared-crops-result.md#inference-probes)). Glimpse sets use
[union pooling with demand](#selection-signal).

- **Several attention heads per label** (hypothesis).
- **Acquisition-count-normalised attention** is tracked in
  [future work](future-work.md#attention-pooling).

#### Rejected, superseded and deferred

- **Rejected:** private fine pooling and label FiLM
  ([label-private](validation/2026-09-16-label-private-crops-result.md)); two crops per label with
  plane spread, also with shared pooling
  ([shared crops](validation/2026-09-16-shared-crops-result.md)); selecting by class-activation
  evidence instead of attention, which lost 0.0006–0.0023 and aimed at bright superficial signal
  ([probe](validation/2026-09-16-label-private-crops-result.md#selection-signal-probe)).
- **Superseded by the glimpse-set design:** a higher Gumbel temperature early in training
  (stochastic selection or the k-DPP); selecting from the separately trained, gated coarse pool
  (the demand head); a study-dependent gate, and a soft per-label bias that shows other labels'
  crops (union pooling, where glimpses have no owning label).
- **Deferred:** per-label learned prototypes as the coarse attention score; weak location labels
  from reports (the strongest signal, but an auxiliary loss); a second selection pass after seeing
  the first crops; light token mixing across coarse tokens (the token graph
  [tied](modeling/active-spatial-token-model.md#retired-token-graph) on the full-triplet model); a
  larger scout ([candidates and costs](research/scout-backbones.md); it already reads the crops'
  pixels at the same spacing, and its pool reached 0.82 with nearly flat attention, so flat maps
  look like a training-signal problem, not capacity).

## Inference optimization

Measured on Kaggle's two T4s with the champion; the
[profiling record](inference/2026-09-15-kaggle-inference-profiling.md) owns the evidence. The
submitted runtime already uses 16 loader workers and FP16 with fixed 64-image channels-last
backbone chunks ([submission](inference/2026-09-15-fast-champion-submission.md), 0.936 public).
Loading dominates: DICOM slice-header reads over NFS are 72% of cold loading time.

### Runtime changes (outside the preprocessing identity)

- **Main-thread warm-up** is implemented in the checkout and ships with the next submission: the
  worker indexes in a background thread while the main thread loads the model and runs one zero
  chunk (first FP16 batch 1.1–1.8 s instead of 26–34 s; worker wall 318 s versus 326 s).
  A learned-zoom submission also needs the scout chunked and warmed, since its canvas count
  varies per batch; ReXNet-200 chunks are already warmed at both glimpse sizes.
- **Torch-free coordinator:** `predict.py` imports the model stack only to count GPUs and read
  study IDs. Count GPUs with `nvidia-smi -L` or `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` and read the template and
  series CSVs without importing `inference.py` (`resolve_raw_dir` lives in `dataset.py`, so moving
  it is an identity change). Coordinator imports take 35–55 s before `main`; at the efficiency
  formula's 32,400 s scale, 30 s is 0.00093 of the score.

### Loading code changes (identity changes, ship with a retrain)

These edit `dataset.py` and `preprocessing/`, so they change the preprocessing identity and ship
with a retrain. Costs are from the sequential 100-study loading profile (cold, then warm OS cache);
file names are SOP instance UIDs, so every slice header must still be read to order slices.
Validate each change locally on CPU: every train series must produce identical `IndexedSeries`
records, selections and prepared tensors.

1. **Index only the worker's studies** (`build_series_index`, `prediction_data`). The index covers
   the whole split although each worker predicts half of it: pass the worker's study IDs and filter
   the series CSV before touching disk. Measured: 24–64 s per worker for 904 studies.
2. **List each series directory once** (`build_series_index`, `index_series`; series listing 3.1 s
   cold). Without the image-count parquet, as on Kaggle, the index globs `*.dcm` only to order
   candidates, and `index_series` globs the same directory again. Take counts from one listing,
   keeping the `(image_count, uid)` order and the `KNOWN_BAD_SERIES` adjustment.
3. **Minimal header parser** (`read_header`; slice headers 146 s cold, 35 s warm, 19,191 files).
   pydicom `read_partial` costs 1.8 ms of CPU per header, 46% of warm loading CPU. Read one fixed
   prefix per file (extended only if the pixel-data element lies beyond it) and scan explicit-VR
   little-endian elements for the tags `DicomHeader` uses: image position and orientation, pixel
   spacing, slice location, instance number, rows, columns, bits stored, pixel representation,
   rescale slope and intercept, transfer syntax and the pixel-data offset and length. Fall back to
   pydicom for any other encoding.
4. **Threaded header reads within a series** (`index_series`). NFS latency is 111 s of the 146 s
   cold header cost: read the series' prefixes with a small thread pool, then parse in order. Raw
   reads scaled from 96 to 872 files/s with 16 threads; parsing holds the GIL, so this needs
   change 3 to pay off.
5. **Threaded frame reads within a series** (`decode_series`, `read_frame`; frame reads 25.5 s cold,
   12.5 s warm, 11,930 frames). Each selected slice reopens its file and reads the pixel payload
   serially; read the unique slices' payloads with the same thread pool (13 s of the cost is NFS
   latency), keeping failure handling per path.
6. **Resample a series in one pass** (`resample_crop`; 8.2 s). Every slice runs two scipy sparse
   products; slices of one series share shape and spacing, so apply the cached axis filters to the
   stacked frames at once, and apply rescale before stacking.
7. **Cheaper exact intensity bounds and normalization** (`_linear_quantiles`, `_normalize_pixels`;
   7.6 s). Partitioning concatenates every valid resampled pixel of an acquisition; use exact
   histogram-assisted selection (the [future-work](future-work.md#data-preparation) comparison)
   and fold the clip, shift, scale and rounding passes together.
8. **Profile geometry and triplet sampling** (`estimate_reference`, `project_reference`,
   `build_pool`, `select_triplets`; 7.0 s, unchanged when warm, so pure CPU) before choosing a
   change; scipy `truncnorm`, `logsumexp` and `linear_sum_assignment` run per slot.
9. **GPU resampling and normalization** only if CPU still limits after 3–8: decode raw frames on
   CPU, then crop-resample, clip and normalize on the mostly idle GPUs. This changes numerics.

Rough sequential estimate if 3–7 each remove most of their measured cost: 2.03 s per cold study
falls toward 0.5–0.6 s, after which the two T4s' forward pass, not loading, would set throughput.

Do not pursue: autocast FP16 with variable shapes, more than 16 loader workers, notebook
pre-reading of DICOMs or packages, a local bytecode cache, or TPU notebooks (not allowed for
submission).
